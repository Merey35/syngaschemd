Introduction

Who discovered this process?

Syngas, or synthesis gas, is a mixture mainly composed of carbon monoxide (CO) and hydrogen (H₂), produced through the gasification of carbon-based materials such as coal, biomass or natural gas at high temperatures with limited oxygen or steam (Inayat et al., 2024). This process converts solid or liquid feedstocks into a combustible gaseous fuel.
The origins of syngas date back to the early 19th century during the Industrial Revolution, when scientists began exploring methods of converting solid fuels into gas. Sir William Grove’s (fig.1) experiments in the 1830s on gas reactions and electrochemical processes provided early scientific understanding of hydrogen and carbon monoxide formation, forming the basis for gasification technologies. In the early 20th century, Franz Fischer (fig.2) and Hans Tropsch (fig.3) significantly advanced syngas applications by developing a catalytic process to convert syngas into liquid hydrocarbons, known as the Fischer–Tropsch synthesis .
Syngas gained industrial importance in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with the construction of coal gasification plants for lighting and heating. Its strategic value increased during World War II, when syngas-based synthetic fuels were used to offset shortages of petroleum resources (Inayat et al., 2024). Today, syngas research focuses on sustainable energy production using renewable feedstocks such as biomass, waste and carbon dioxide.

What is the product and why is this product important?

The main product of this process is synthesis gas (syngas), a combustible gas mixture primarily composed of carbon monoxide (CO) and hydrogen (H₂), with smaller amounts of methane (CH₄), carbon dioxide (CO₂), and nitrogen (N₂). Syngas is an extremely important product because it functions as both an energy source and a chemical feedstock. From an energy perspective, syngas can be burned directly to generate electricity and heat or further processed into liquid fuels using the Fischer–Tropsch synthesis, which produces diesel and other hydrocarbons. From an industrial chemistry perspective, syngas is a key raw material in the production of methanol, ammonia, and synthetic fuels, which are essential for fertilizers, plastics, and pharmaceuticals. Its importance lies in its flexibility and sustainability. Unlike fossil fuels, syngas can be produced from renewable biomass, making it a critical intermediate in reducing reliance on non-renewable energy sources and supporting long-term energy security.

What global problem does it help to solve?

Synthesis of syngas helps to challenge the problem of the greenhouse gas effect and lower the amount of agricultural waste, as it can be used as a renewable energy source, which could replace non-renewable fuels while reducing waste. This study will focus on the biomass gasification as a process for syngas production. As biomass is a renewable resource, the process of syngas synthesis is considered carbon neutral, which further promotes its role in decreasing the greenhouse gas effect (reneenergy. com, 2023).
