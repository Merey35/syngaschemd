Introduction

Who discovered this process?

Syngas, or synthesis gas, is a mixture mainly composed of carbon monoxide (CO) and hydrogen (H₂), produced through the gasification of carbon-based materials such as coal, biomass or natural gas at high temperatures with limited oxygen or steam (Inayat et al., 2024). This process converts solid or liquid feedstocks into a combustible gaseous fuel. The origins of syngas date back to the early 19th century during the Industrial Revolution, when scientists began exploring methods of converting solid fuels into gas. Sir William Grove’s (fig.1) experiments in the 1830s on gas reactions and electrochemical processes provided early scientific understanding of hydrogen and carbon monoxide formation, forming the basis for gasification technologies. In the early 20th century, Franz Fischer (fig.2) and Hans Tropsch (fig.3) significantly advanced syngas applications by developing a catalytic process to convert syngas into liquid hydrocarbons, known as the Fischer–Tropsch synthesis . Syngas gained industrial importance in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with the construction of coal gasification plants for lighting and heating. Its strategic value increased during World War II, when syngas-based synthetic fuels were used to offset shortages of petroleum resources (Inayat et al., 2024). Today, syngas research focuses on sustainable energy production using renewable feedstocks such as biomass, waste and carbon dioxide.
